    Quiz¶

    Assessment Quiz (20 questions)¶

    This quiz covers Lessons 1–5 of Supporting Scripture Pre-publishing Checks. You need 80% (16/20) to pass.

    Questions are weighted toward the course's core objectives (confirming the parallel- passage check and the numbers/weights/measures check(s) available for a team's Paratext version, formatting diagnosis, glossary/layout, and the final read-through and escalation judgment), with the secondary objectives (false-clean recognition, incomplete/stale Biblical Terms coverage, Project Biblical Terms bloat, and the layout/hyphenation decision) still represented. This quiz can assess recognition and diagnostic reasoning — it can't assess live coaching. The mentor-reviewed scenario bank (06-scenario-bank.md) is what earns this course's 3 - Independent claim.

    Section 1: Workflow, Role, and the False-Clean Trap (Questions 1-3)¶

    Question 1: A team says every check in Paratext shows clean and they're ready for the typesetter. What is the FIRST thing you should do? - A) Sign off and send the files on, since a clean status in every check means the work is done - B) Escalate to an LT mentor straight away so a more experienced consultant can verify the checks - C) Ask how the checks came to be clean, to confirm they were genuinely reviewed and not just cleared - D) Rerun only the wordlist check, since that is the only check that can hide problems

    Question 2: A team disagrees about whether a parallel passage should be harmonized — a genuine content question about Scripture. Who owns that decision, and who would you escalate to if expert review is needed? - A) The team decides; escalate to a Translation Consultant if expert review is needed - B) You decide it yourself, since you're the consultant in the room and can compare the two passages - C) The typesetter decides, since harmonising affects how the passages will lay out on the page - D) The team decides; escalate to an LT mentor if expert review is needed

    Question 3: True or False: A check status showing "no outstanding errors" always means the team has genuinely reviewed and resolved everything. - A) True - B) False

    Section 2: Wordlist and Biblical Terms (Questions 4-8)¶

    Question 4: A team's wordlist status shows zero errors, but thousands of entries were marked correct with none flagged as suspect — including several proper names spelled two different ways, all marked correct. What does this pattern most likely indicate? - A) The wordlist genuinely has no problems, since zero errors is the target at Stage 6 - B) The spelling check malfunctioned and failed to flag the suspect entries - C) The team was working from an outdated copy of the wordlist that predates the team's most recent edits - D) The wordlist was blanket-approved in bulk rather than genuinely reviewed entry by entry

    Question 5: A team tells you their Biblical Terms list is done. You open it and see several rows still highlighted with placeholder text ("Double click to enter rendering(s) from project text") and a Found count of "0" or blank. What does this indicate? - A) A software glitch in the Biblical Terms tool that needs a support ticket raised - B) Those terms are still at their default starting state, with no rendering entered yet - C) The team accidentally deleted those renderings, which now need restoring from a project backup - D) The terms were resolved, and the blank display is a known quirk you can safely ignore

    Question 6: While reviewing a Biblical Terms list, you notice one term has several candidate renderings listed, but the Found count shows only one of them as actually present in the current text. What should the team do? - A) Review the term's rendering list, confirm which is current, and remove the stale ones - B) Leave all the renderings in place, since extra renderings help Paratext find more occurrences - C) Delete the term from the Biblical Terms list entirely, then add it back with a single rendering - D) Run Guess Renderings again so Paratext can pick the correct rendering automatically

    Question 7: A team reports Send/Receive has become noticeably slower, and mentions they added the entire "All Biblical Terms" reference list to the Project's Biblical Terms list. What is the most likely cause, and what should you advise? - A) The slowdown is unrelated to the terms list; check their internet connection speed first - B) Paratext's installation is likely corrupted; advise reinstalling it to restore normal performance - C) Viewing any large list slows Send/Receive; advise closing the list before syncing - D) Adding the full list bloated the project; advise right-sizing it to the terms actually used

    Question 8: Per the SME field case, why was the connection between adding a large Biblical Terms list and the Send/Receive slowdown "hard to convince the team of"? - A) The relationship isn't real, and the slowdown turned out to have a different cause - B) It only happens on certain operating systems, so the team couldn't reproduce it - C) The team didn't believe that adding terms to a list could affect Send/Receive performance - D) The team had already right-sized the list, so it seemed the list couldn't be the cause

    Section 3: Parallel Passages and Measures (Questions 9-12)¶

    Question 9: What is "over-harmonising" in the context of parallel-passage checks? - A) Leaving parallel passages unreviewed so that differences between them go unchecked - B) Making parallel passages match more closely than the original texts do - C) Approving parallel passages in bulk without opening the Show differences comparison first - D) Adding so many cross-references that the parallel passages become hard to follow

    Question 10: Two accounts of the same event have several differences in wording. The team lead says, "those are just supposed to be different — we're not touching them." You open the Parallel Passages tool and see the relevant verses still showing a red "?" in the Status column, meaning they were edited during a major revision three weeks ago and haven't been reviewed since. What should you do? - A) Escalate the differences to a Translation Consultant without discussing them with the team - B) Accept the team lead's explanation and mark the check done, since some variation is legitimate - C) Compare the two accounts yourself and decide whether the wording should match - D) Ask the team to review the verses still showing a red "?" in the Parallel Passages tool

    Question 11: A new, consolidated Paratext check is expected to combine numbers, weights, and measures checking into one check, replacing the older, separate Numbers check — but not every team's Paratext version has migrated to it yet. Before accepting any numbers/measures check results, what should you confirm? - A) That the results match your own sense of how the numbers and measures should be rendered - B) That the old and new checks both appear together as one combined list in the Open Biblical Terms List dialog - C) Which check(s) the team's version actually has, and that it ran against their documented approach - D) That the typesetter has reviewed the results, since numbers often affect the layout of tables

    Question 12: A team's Paratext version has only the older Numbers check and no working Measures check. At your request, the team reviews their own measure renderings and finds two occurrences of the same measurement rendered differently. Nobody is sure a documented approach to measures exists. What should you do? - A) Settle whether a documented approach exists, then refer the inconsistency to the team - B) Compare the measure renderings yourself to confirm the team's finding before acting on it - C) Decide which of the two renderings is correct and ask the team to match the other to it - D) Ask the typesetter to make the two occurrences consistent when the files are composed

    Section 4: Formatting and References (Questions 13-14)¶

    Question 13: Why should formatting checks be worked "structural-first" — Chapter/Verse Numbers and marker pairs before headings, titles, and references? - A) Paratext's design requires it: the later checks won't run until the earlier ones have passed - B) One upstream break, like an unclosed marker pair, can cause many downstream-looking errors - C) The typesetter requires this order, and expects the checks to be reported to them in this sequence - D) Headings, titles, and references are harder to fix, so they're best left until last

    Question 14: A team says their Punctuation Inventory "was already checked back when we started the project," but the project has since gone through two more revision passes and is headed to a typesetter in three weeks. What should you do? - A) Skip it, since the Punctuation Inventory belongs to PTXprint rather than Paratext's own checks - B) Accept it as done, since punctuation rarely changes once a project's early drafting is finished - C) Leave it for the typesetter, who will catch any punctuation problems during composition - D) Ask them to open and genuinely review the Punctuation Inventory now, including its settings

    Section 5: Glossary Linking and Layout (Questions 15-18)¶

    Question 15: What is the correct glossary-linking scope, and what is the common field mistake? - A) Link every occurrence of a term; the mistake is linking too few occurrences - B) Link only the first occurrence in each book; the mistake is linking it again in later chapters - C) Link the first occurrence per section; the mistake is over-linking every occurrence - D) Link terms only in footnotes; the mistake is linking terms in the running text

    Question 16: You notice a high-frequency term is linked at nearly every occurrence across several chapters. What should you do? - A) Unlink the over-applied marks and relink at "first occurrence in every section" - B) Delete the glossary entry for that term so the excess links no longer point anywhere - C) Leave it as it is, since more links help readers who start reading mid-chapter - D) Relink the term at "all occurrences" again so every instance is marked consistently

    Question 17: An expat project admin wants single-column layout because the language has long words and the team has never used hyphenation. What should you check before agreeing? - A) Whether single-column is technically easier to set up in PTXprint for this project - B) Which layout the LWC (Language of Wider Communication) Bibles the community already reads use - C) Nothing further, since long words and a team with no hyphenation experience settle the decision on their own - D) Whether the typesetter prefers single-column for a language with long words

    Question 18: The community currently reads two-column Bibles in the LWC (Language of Wider Communication), and the target language has long words. What concrete technical step should you take? - A) Reduce the font size in PTXprint until the long words fit the column width - B) Recommend the community switch to single-column, since long words won't fit well in two columns - C) Enable Show hyphenation in the Wordlist and get the team to review and correct the auto-generated hyphenation breaks - D) Turn on word break characters under Project settings > Language settings > Other Characters so long words can break across columns

    Section 6: The Final Read-Through and Escalation (Questions 19-20)¶

    Question 19: During the final PTXprint draft-PDF read-through, what should you be watching for? - A) Spelling errors in the source text, so you can list the words the team needs to correct - B) Whether each Biblical Terms rendering in the PDF is the accurate one for that term - C) Whether the wordlist and Basic Checks have been re-run since the PDF was last generated - D) Spreads, orphan words, footnote shifts, heading placement, and underfilled pages

    Question 20: During a draft-PDF read-through you spot an orphan word, a footnote shifted two pages from its reference, and a heading landing awkwardly at a column break. What is your role regarding these findings? - A) Resolve what's yours to fix, and triage the rest as the team's call or the typesetter's - B) Note them but leave them alone, since anything in the draft PDF is entirely the typesetter's job - C) Fix all three yourself in PTXprint straight away, since you're the one who found them - D) Escalate all three to a Translation Consultant, since they affect how Scripture is presented

    Answer key¶
