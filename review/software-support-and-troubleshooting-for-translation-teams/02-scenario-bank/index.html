    Scenario bank¶

    Estimated time: 15 minutes

    Purpose: Applied practice scenarios you work through after the lesson content, before the quiz. Each one asks you to explain how you would approach a real support situation using the 5-step framework from the lesson (Clarify the Problem → Understand the Context → Reproduce the Problem → Test Systematically → Know When to Escalate). At the Has Knowledge level, the goal is to show clear, systematic thinking on paper — not to fix a live machine — so your written reasoning is the deliverable.

    Format: the scenarios progress from foundational → intermediate → complex. Scenario F1 is also worked as the in-lesson Challenge; F1 and I1 are confirmed real field cases.

    How to use these scenarios¶

    For learners¶

    • Read each scenario carefully.
    • Apply the 5-step troubleshooting framework from the lesson.
    • Write out your reasoning — the questions you would ask, the order you would work in, and why. Explaining your approach is the task; you are not expected to have a single "right" fix.
    • Submit your written responses to your mentor for feedback.

    For mentors¶

    • Use the accompanying mentor guide for assessment criteria.
    • Look for systematic thinking, not just a "correct" answer.
    • Provide constructive feedback.

    INFO — a note on realism. Scenarios F1 and I1 are confirmed situations the course's SME has seen in the field. Scenario C1 is illustrative and plausible but is not a confirmed field case — treat it as a realistic thinking exercise rather than a documented incident.

    Foundational scenarios¶

    Scenario F1: The Panicked Translator¶

    Situation: You receive this message: "Everything is gone! I opened my project this morning and all my work from the past week has disappeared! What happened?!"

    Context: The translator is on a deadline, working from a field location, and clearly distressed. You do not yet know anything about how their project is set up or what changed between yesterday and today.

    Objectives exercised: 1.

    Your task:

    1. Write out the first three clarifying questions you would ask to understand what is actually happening.
    2. Explain what information you would need before proposing any solution.
    3. Note how you would acknowledge the translator's stress while you gather that information.

    Intermediate scenarios¶

    Scenario I1: The Unclear Error¶

    Situation: A translator sends you a screenshot of an error message in a language you don't speak well, and says "I keep getting this error and I don't know what to do."

    Context: You cannot easily read the error text, and you are supporting the translator remotely rather than sitting beside them.

    Objectives exercised: 3.

    Your task:

    1. Describe your troubleshooting approach — what would you do first, second, and third?
    2. Explain how you would gather reliable information when you can't easily read the error yourself (for example, by having the translator show you the problem happening rather than relying only on their description).
    3. Explain why directly observing or reproducing the problem is more dependable here than working from the translator's account alone.

    Complex scenarios¶

    Scenario C1: The Recurring Problem¶

    Situation: A translator contacts you saying "This keeps happening. Every few days my work gets stuck and I have to restart the program. Can you fix it permanently?"

    Context: The problem is intermittent — it appears every few days rather than every time — which makes it harder to catch in the act. You will need to reason about what is "normal" for this team's workflow and how you would isolate an on-again, off-again fault.

    Objectives exercised: 2, 4, 5.

    Your task:

    1. Explain how you would approach diagnosing an intermittent problem, and what project- and workflow-context factors you would want to understand first (how the team works, how they collaborate, what recently changed) to judge what is normal versus abnormal.
    2. Describe the information you would gather to identify a pattern, and how you would test systematically — changing one thing at a time — rather than trying fixes at random.
    3. Explain what you would tell the translator about your process, including how you would recognise that the problem exceeds what you can resolve alone and what the appropriate next step would be (escalate, contact vendor support, restore from backup, or agree a workaround).
