# Consultant's Triage Card

The **Consultant's Triage Card** below must be filled out for each scenario you work, alongside
your written diagnostic plan. Used in Module 2 §2.5, Module 3 §3.4, and Module 4 §4.3 and §4.4.

The order of the questions is deliberate. The first three can make everything after them
irrelevant: if the data isn't safe, if there's a safety hazard, or if the disk is encrypted and
nobody has the key, those get resolved before diagnosis continues.

> **Scenario ID:** ________
>
> 1. **Data Status:** Is the data backed up? (Yes/No/Unknown)
>    - If it's irreplaceable and not backed up, getting it off comes before any further testing.
> 2. **Safety Risk — any stop sign present?** (Yes/No)
>    - Swelling (bulging case, stiff or raised trackpad, laptop rocks on a flat surface)?
>    - Smoke, or a burning/sharp/ozone smell?
>    - Visible damage to the power adapter (fraying, cracks, scorch marks)?
>    - Any "Yes" ends troubleshooting and removes the machine from service.
> 3. **Encryption:** Is the drive encrypted (BitLocker/FileVault)? Who holds the recovery key?
>    - "Unknown" is a finding. A working laptop with an unreadable disk is a total data loss.
> 4. **The "Golden Test":** What one test isolates the problem fastest? (e.g., External Monitor,
>    Disable Touchpad, Run On Battery, BIOS/UEFI Setup Screen, Linux Live USB, Plugged-in vs Battery)
> 5. **Serviceability:** Is the affected part actually replaceable on this model? (RAM/storage
>    soldered? Battery glued?) Check before recommending any internal work.
> 6. **Power Profile:** What is the site's power like, and what protects the machine?
>    (Steady / sags / outages / generator / inverter; surge strip / AVR / UPS / nothing; earthed outlets?)
> 7. **The Workaround:** If a part takes 2 months to arrive, how does the user work tomorrow?
